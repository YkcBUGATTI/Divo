The Beginning

Birth

In August 2018, at The Quail in Monterey, Bugatti unveiled the Divo.

New CEO Stephan Winkelmann had just taken the helm and made a call: straight-line speed alone wasn't enough. Chiron was already the fastest production car on Earth. Customers wanted something else — the thrill of a corner.

So the Divo was born. Same W16 heart, but the chassis, aerodynamics, and weight were all reworked. Top speed dropped from 420+ to 380 km/h — those 40 km/h bought 90 kg of extra downforce and 8 seconds off the Nardò handling circuit.

The car is named after Albert Divo, a French racing driver who won the Targa Florio twice for Bugatti in the 1920s and '30s — a 148 km endurance race through the mountain roads of Sicily, with no guardrails, only stone walls and cliffs.

The Divo also marks Bugatti's return to coachbuilding. 40 units, no two alike. Each one a bespoke collaboration between customer and designer, starting from a blank page.

"

To date, a modern Bugatti has represented a perfect balance between high performance, straight-line dynamics and luxury. With the Divo, we are now shifting the focus to lateral dynamics.

Stephan Winkelmann · President, Bugatti

03
Design

Design

Design lead Jascha Straub gave his team a simple brief: the Divo must be instantly recognizable as a Bugatti, but impossible to confuse with a Chiron.

The front end is entirely new. The horseshoe grille is wider, flanked by new air inlets for brake cooling. The vertical LED headlights are just 35 mm tall — the slimmest ever on a Bugatti production car.

The roof is shaped as a NACA duct, channeling air along a central fin that runs from front to rear wing. That fin is a direct nod to the Type 57 Atlantic — arguably the most beautiful car Bugatti ever made.

At the rear, the 3D-printed taillight steals the show: 44 fins of varying size, wider at the outer edge, narrower toward the center, creating a gradual fade when illuminated. The entire rear grille is produced by a 3D printing process.

Aerodynamics

Aerodynamics

The Divo's aero package isn't a few carbon fiber canards bolted on. The entire front cover, front spoiler, side skirts, rear wing, and diffuser were redesigned from scratch. One goal: downforce.

The result: 456 kg of total downforce, 90 kg more than the Chiron. That translates directly into grip through corners.

Interior

Interior

08   Divo Racing Blue dominates the driver's side; the passenger side stays dark with selective accents.

The cabin carries the exterior's two-tone logic inside. The driver's side is almost entirely Divo Racing Blue — a bright turquoise shade developed exclusively for this car. The passenger side stays dark, split cleanly down the center console.

The seats are a new design with stronger lateral support, thinner and lighter backs, but no compromise on comfort. Carbon fiber door panels feature the same diamond pattern as the exterior.

Alcantara, carbon trim, Divo Grey leather — every interior material mirrors the exterior spec. Each car's colorway is signed off individually between customer and design team.

06
Bespoke

Bespoke

40 Divos. No two the same.

Bugatti pulled the word "coachbuilding" out of the museum. Historically, a coachbuilder would craft bodywork over a customer's chosen chassis. With the Divo, the collaboration goes from paint color to stitch direction — every detail, co-developed.

The "Lady Bug" is the most extreme example. The customer wanted a geometric gradient diamond pattern across the entire body. The design team spent a year and a half finding a technical solution: six-meter transfer films applied piece by piece, each one bubble-free, then painted over without damaging the pattern.

“If it bears comparison with another car, it is not a Bugatti.”

Ettore Bugatti · Founder

Deliveries took place at The Thermal Club in California. Three Divos gathered one last time before going to their owners — French Racing Blue, Titanium Silver Matt, Argent Crystal Gloss — three completely different personalities, one chassis.

The final Divo delivered in Europe wore the classic Bugatti EB color as its primary. The owner has a sharp instinct for tradition.

Targa Florio

Targa Florio

Albert Divo joined the Bugatti works team in 1928 — and won the Targa Florio that same year, then again the next. The course followed roughly 148 km of public mountain roads through Sicily's Madonie range, with around 1,400 corners and no guardrails, only stone walls and cliffs.

He drove a Type 35 B. Between 1925 and 1929, the Type 35 won the Targa Florio five times in a row for Bugatti — the fastest cornering car of its era.

In 2022, Bugatti drove the Divo back to that circuit, joined by a Type 35. Two cars, 90 years apart, reunited where Albert Divo made his name.

“I am deeply impressed by how the Divo — designed for much higher speeds — is able to get to grips with these roads, some of which are in very poor condition, and the very short distances between corners.”

Andy Wallace · Test driver, Bugatti

The Divo never chased a straight-line record. It pursues a different kind of fast — lateral fast. 40 units, each one different: not a mass-produced product, but a one-to-one conversation between person and machine.

"

The modern interpretation of coachbuilding gave us engineers new freedom.

Stefan Ellrott · Head of Technical Development, Bugatti
